Kello Alpha has built-in playbooks for three jobs that go beyond a plain search: reference checks, event guest lists, and founders and talent moves. No special words are needed. Kello Alpha recognises the playbook from what you ask and shows the evidence behind each person it suggests.

## Reference checks

Kello Alpha runs reference checks by finding everyone whose time at each employer overlapped with the candidate's, ranking who knew them best, and giving you a who-to-call list with the reason for each person. It also checks the candidate's résumé and supplied references against real employment history, and preps tailored questions for each call.

Call the people who actually worked with them, not just the ones they picked.

### How do I start a reference check?

Give Alpha the candidate in any form:

- A LinkedIn link
- Name and company
- Résumé text, pasted in
- Someone from a list you already have

```text
I want to do a reference check on https://www.linkedin.com/in/candidate-example
```

### Who does Alpha suggest I call?

Everyone whose time at an employer overlapped with the candidate's, ranked by how well they likely knew them:

- **Likely manager**
- **Peers** on the same team or function
- **Reports**, if the candidate managed people
- **Cross-functional partners** they would have worked with

Each person comes with the reason they're on the list, such as the overlap dates and how their roles connected.

Alpha doesn't provide contact details. Reach people through your own network. If someone at your company worked with a reference, the profile's **Connections at your org** section shows that tie.

### Does Alpha check the résumé?

Yes. Alpha compares the résumé against real employment history and flags:

- **Title inflation**: a title on the résumé that's bigger than the one on record.
- **Shifted dates**: start or end dates that don't line up.
- **Missing employers**: jobs on record that the résumé leaves out.

Treat each flag as a question to ask, not a verdict. There's often a simple explanation.

### Can Alpha check the references the candidate gave me?

Yes. Paste the names or links. Alpha checks whether each person actually overlapped with the candidate.

- **No real overlap**: flagged, so you know the claimed working relationship can't be found.
- **Real but close ties**, such as old batchmates or people who moved companies together: kept, with a "how to ask" note so you can get a candid view.

### How do I prepare for a call?

Ask Alpha to prep you:

```text
Prep me for the call with [reference name]
```

You get up to five questions tailored to how that person worked with the candidate. They're designed so the answer isn't predictable. You won't get "was she a strong performer?". You'll get questions that draw out specific examples and comparisons.

### A simple reference-check flow

<Steps>
  <Step title="Find who overlapped">
    Give Alpha the candidate. Review the who-to-call list and the reasons.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the record">
    Look at any résumé flags. Note what to ask about.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check supplied references">
    Paste the references the candidate gave you and see which overlaps hold up.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Prep each call">
    Ask "prep me for the call with" each person you'll speak to.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Event guest lists

Kello Alpha builds guest lists for meetups, dinners and conferences. Describe the event or paste a link to its page, and Alpha works out the audience, then ranks people by closeness to the venue, role fit and company fit. It can also audit a list you already have.

### How do I build an invite list?

Tell Alpha about the event. A sentence works. A pasted event brief or a link to the event page works better, because Alpha can read it.

```text
Who should we invite to our testing meetup in Indiranagar?
```

```text
Find me the right people to invite to this event: [event page link]
Deeptech founders who haven't raised yet or just raised early.
```

Alpha pins down the event, builds a profile of the audience, and searches.

### How does Alpha choose who to invite?

It ranks people on three things:

- **Closeness to the venue**: people who can actually come.
- **Role fit**: the kind of work the event is about.
- **Company fit**: the kinds of companies you want in the room.

It also respects team-size floors when you set them: "only people at companies with 50+ engineers".

If your company is a product company hosting the event, Alpha can warm up the list using recent job posts. For example: "34 of the top 100 are at companies whose recent job posts mention Playwright." Those are teams already investing in the topic.

### How do I narrow to the room size?

Say how many seats you have and what to weight. Typical follow-ups:

```text
Narrow to the top 30. Capacity is 30.
```

```text
Weight people at top-tier AI labs in India, with a public presence and 7+ years' experience
```

```text
Near-director or at least manager level only
```

### Can Alpha check a list I already have?

Yes. Paste your list of LinkedIn links and ask Alpha to audit it. It tells you who's worth inviting for this event and why, so you can trim a long list or spot gaps.

```text
Here's our current invite list for the AI founders dinner. Who's worth inviting?
[LinkedIn links]
```

### What do I get at the end?

A saved list of people, plus a CSV if you ask for one. Say "export this as CSV". See [working with results](/alpha/working-with-results). Alpha doesn't provide emails or phone numbers, so send invites through your own channels.

## Founders and talent moves

Kello Alpha finds founders and founders-to-be, ex-founders and acqui-hired teams, people who made a specific career move, and people who worked together at a given time. It shows the evidence behind each person and says how sure it is, because no single signal makes someone a founder.

### How do I find founders to watch?

Ask in plain words. Alpha covers:

- **Founders to watch** and **repeat founders**
- **Early employees at breakout companies**
- **Operators likely to found** something
- **People who just left a hot company**
- **People in stealth**

```text
Show me people in stealth mode who left a top AI lab in the last 30 days
```

```text
Every product manager in India with 4+ years' experience who left their job after March 2026
```

### How does Alpha decide someone is a founder?

Carefully. One signal never labels someone a founder. A "stealth" headline only nominates a person. A live product page or real public work confirms them.

Results come in three tiers, **act now**, **warm** and **watch**, and each person shows the evidence that put them there. Start with act now; keep watch for later.

Education is never used as a gate in founder searches.

### Can Alpha find ex-founders and acqui-hires?

Yes. For example:

```text
Founders who got acqui-hired in the last 3 years
```

```text
Second-time founders in fintech
```

```text
Ex-founders now looking for roles
```

There's no "was acquired" field anywhere, so Alpha infers it from evidence: a whole team moving to one company together, the old company shrinking to zero, the founders' own words. It labels how sure it is and never calls something an acquisition on one signal.

### How do I search for career moves?

Describe the move. Alpha understands "from", "to" and timing.

```text
People who left Google for a startup
```

```text
Engineers who moved from Infosys to a product company
```

```text
People who left Flipkart in the last 6 months
```

### How do I find who worked with whom?

Ask about a person, a company or a time window. This is also how reference checks find the people who actually overlapped with a candidate.

```text
Who worked with this candidate at their last job? [LinkedIn link]
```

```text
People at Stripe during 2019–2021
```

### How do I refine a founder search?

Short follow-ups work well:

```text
Consumer internet only, CXO level
```

```text
Only VPs and chiefs. Exclude investors and board members. Flag anyone with entrepreneurial experience.
```

```text
Who is still there and who has left?
```
