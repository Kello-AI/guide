Recruiting is priced in six common ways: contingency (typically ~15–25% of first-year salary, paid on hire), retained search (often ~25–35%, paid in stages), RPO (an ongoing fee for an outsourced recruiting function), per-seat software, pay-on-hire platforms, and a flat fee per role. Kello uses a flat fee per role: ₹75,000 in India or US$3,000 elsewhere, excluding taxes, the same at every seniority level.

**TL;DR**

- Percentage fees rise with the salary you pay. A flat fee per role doesn't.
- Contingency and pay-on-hire bill only when you hire. Retained, RPO, seats and flat fees bill whether or not you hire.
- On a US$100,000 hire, a 20% contingency fee is US$20,000. Kello's fee is US$3,000.
- Kello invoices when your first list arrives, so you see who Kello found before you pay. No charge if Kello finds nobody.
- The right model depends on volume, seniority, how much of the work your team does, and how much predictability you need.

## What are the main recruiting pricing models?

| Model | How you pay | When you pay | Who does the work |

| Retained search | Often ~25–35% of first-year salary | In stages, usually starting at kick-off | Search firm |
| RPO | Often a monthly fee, sometimes with per-hire fees | Monthly, over a contract of several months or more | An outsourced recruiting team |
| Per-seat software | Per seat per month, often plus credits | Monthly or annually | Your team |

These are general market descriptions. Actual terms vary by firm, country and seniority.

## How does contingency recruiting work?

A contingency agency is paid only if you hire someone it introduced, typically around 15–25% of that person's first-year salary. There's no cost if you don't hire. The trade-off is that the fee scales with salary, the final number isn't known until offer stage, and the agency earns more if you hire a more expensive candidate. Agreements often apply the fee if you hire an introduced candidate within a set period afterwards.

## How does retained search work?

Retained search is usually used for senior and executive roles. The firm is paid often around 25–35% of first-year salary, in stages, such as at kick-off, at shortlist and at hire. It's typically exclusive. You pay for a dedicated, thorough search whether or not it ends in a hire.

## How does RPO work?

Recruitment process outsourcing (RPO) means an outside firm runs part or all of your recruiting, often with recruiters embedded in your team. Pricing is often a monthly management fee, sometimes plus per-hire fees, over a contract of several months or more. It suits steady, high-volume hiring more than a few specific roles.

## How do per-seat sourcing tools charge?

Self-serve sourcing and search tools are usually priced per seat per month, often with credits for contact details or exports. The cost doesn't depend on salary, but your team does the searching, reviewing and outreach, so the real cost includes their hours.

## What is pay-on-hire?

Pay-on-hire is any model that bills only when you hire, either a fixed amount or a percentage. Contingency is the most common form. It shifts risk to the provider, which tends to show up as a higher price per hire.

## What is a flat fee per role?

A flat fee per role is one fixed price for sourcing a specific opening, regardless of seniority or salary. Kello charges ₹75,000 per role in India or US$3,000 elsewhere, excluding taxes. One fee covers 3 months from your first list: up to 5 lists of up to 20 people each, outreach to everyone you approve, first conversations with people who say yes, contact details for any candidate, and reference-check guidance.

## What are the honest trade-offs?

**Paying on hire vs paying per role.** Contingency costs nothing if you don't hire. Kello bills per role, so you pay whether or not you hire. Kello limits that risk in three ways: you see the first list before you're invoiced, there's no charge if Kello finds nobody, and if you said Yes to people and Kello couldn't connect you with even one candidate, the fee is returned.

**Predictability.** A percentage fee is known only at offer stage. A flat fee is known the day you send the role. That makes budgets simpler, especially across many roles of different seniority.

**Incentives.** A percentage fee rewards a higher salary. A flat fee means Kello earns nothing more if you hire a more expensive candidate, so it never has a reason to push one.

**How much work stays with you.** Agencies and RPO take on more of the process, including closing. Per-seat tools leave the most work with your team. Kello takes on sourcing and outreach; interviews, offers and closing stay with you.

**Ownership.** With Kello you keep everyone it finds and their contact details, with no placement fee, even if you hire later. Kello doesn't ask for exclusivity.

## Which pricing model should you choose?

- **A few senior, sensitive searches:** retained search is often the right call.
- **Occasional roles where you'd rather pay only on success:** contingency.
- **Large, steady hiring volume:** RPO or an in-house team with sourcing tools.
- **Several specific roles, with a budget you want to know up front:** a flat fee per role.

You can mix models: an agency for one executive search, Kello for the rest.
