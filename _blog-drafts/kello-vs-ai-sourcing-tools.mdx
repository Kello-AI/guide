Self-serve AI sourcing tools give your team a search database, usually priced per seat per month plus credits for contact details, and your team writes the searches and runs outreach. Kello is done-for-you: you send a job description, Kello returns a reasoned list of up to 20 people in about two hours, and a named Kello person reaches out to the people you approve, for one flat fee per role.

Last reviewed: October 2026

**TL;DR**

- AI sourcing tools: you do the work, faster. Kello: Kello does the sourcing work, you make the decisions.
- Tools are usually priced per seat per month, often with credits for contact details or exports. Kello is ₹75,000 per role in India or US$3,000 elsewhere, excluding taxes, with no seats and no credits.
- With a tool, your team writes outreach from its own accounts. With Kello, a named Kello person reaches out from their own LinkedIn, email and WhatsApp.
- Kello shows why each person fits your role and how it approached the search, and learns from your Yes and No marks.

## What's the real difference?

**Who spends the hours.** A tool makes a recruiter faster. Someone still has to turn the job description into searches, read dozens of profiles, decide who fits and write messages. Kello does that part and gives you a list to judge.

**Seats vs roles.** Per-seat pricing makes sense if your team searches every day. Per-role pricing makes sense if what you need is people for specific openings. Kello's list link can be opened and marked by anyone on your team, with no per-seat pricing.

**Matching vs reading.** Many tools match profiles to the criteria you write. Kello starts with your company and the kind of people you've hired, then reads each profile for meaning: what each employer does, company stage when they joined and left, early promotions, whether your role is a step up for them. It looks for several kinds of people who could succeed, so the list isn't one profile repeated 20 times.

**Who runs outreach.** With Kello, outreach is part of the role fee. A named person reaches out, with AI giving them context on each candidate so the message fits the person. In India, that includes WhatsApp, where most candidates reply.

## When is a self-serve tool the better choice?

- **You have dedicated sourcers** who search all day and want direct control over every query.
- **You need to explore**, not fill a role: market maps, competitor teams, event lists, investor research.
- **You want outreach in your own voice** from your own accounts.
- **You run many similar searches** where the query is well understood.

If that sounds like your team, Kello also has a self-serve search app, [Kello Alpha](/alpha/overview), where you search 830M+ professionals in plain language.

## When is Kello the better choice?

- You have more open roles than hours to source them.
- You want a list you can review in minutes, with reasons, rather than results to sift.
- You want outreach handled and only interested candidates who meet your must-haves brought to you.
- You want a predictable cost per role with no seats or credits to manage.

## FAQ

### Is Kello an AI sourcing tool?

Kello is an AI sourcing agent, not a self-serve tool. You don't search; you send a role and get back a list with reasons. Kello also runs outreach on your behalf.

### Do I pay for contact details with Kello?

No. Contact details for any candidate you want to reach yourself are included in the role fee, with no credits. Tell Kello when you reach out, so candidates hear from one of you, not both.

### Can my whole team use Kello without seats?

Yes. Share the list link with your hiring manager or anyone involved. Anyone with the link can review and mark candidates.

### Can I use Kello alongside a sourcing tool?

Yes. Kello doesn't ask for exclusivity. If someone is already in your pipeline, tell Kello or mark them No and they won't be contacted.

### Does Kello train on my data?

No. Your job description, feedback and data are used only to get your own lists right. They're never used to train Kello's algorithms and never shared with other customers.
