An AI sourcing agent is a service that takes your job brief and returns a list of people who fit the role, each with the reasons they fit, doing the searching and reading a sourcer would do instead of handing you a search tool. Kello is an AI sourcing agent: you email a job description, and about two hours later you get a list of up to 20 people with why each one fits your role.

**TL;DR**

- An AI sourcing agent does the work of a sourcer: understand the role, find people, read their profiles, explain the fit.
- You give it a brief, not a search query. It decides where and how to look.
- Good ones show their reasoning for each person and learn from your feedback.
- Some, like Kello, also reach out to candidates on your behalf and set up first conversations.
- Pricing varies by category. Kello charges a flat fee per role, with the first role free.

## How is an AI sourcing agent different from a search tool?

A search tool gives you a database and a search box. You write the query, scan the results, decide who fits and run outreach yourself. The tool is fast, but the judgement and the hours are yours.

An AI sourcing agent starts from the brief. It works out what the role really needs, looks for people, reads each profile for meaning, and hands you a short list with reasons. Your time goes into deciding who to talk to, not into building searches.

## How is it different from a recruitment agency?

A recruitment agency is a team of people who source, screen and present candidates, usually paid a percentage of the hire's salary when you hire. An AI sourcing agent does the sourcing work with AI, typically much faster, and is often priced differently. Kello, for example, charges one flat fee per role regardless of the salary.

The two can work side by side. Kello doesn't ask for exclusivity. See [AI sourcing agent vs recruitment agency](/learn/ai-sourcing-agent-vs-recruitment-agency) for a full comparison.

## What does a good AI sourcing agent do?

A useful test is to ask whether it does what a strong senior sourcer would do:

- **Understands your company first.** What you do, how you've grown, and the kind of people you've hired.
- **Reads profiles, not keywords.** What each past employer actually does, how big it was when the person joined and left, whether they were promoted early, what they worked on.
- **Asks whether the person would say yes.** A role that's a step down in title or scope rarely lands.
- **Shows its reasoning.** Each person comes with why they fit your role, so you can check the logic.
- **Learns from feedback.** Your Yes and No marks change the next list.
- **Avoids personal traits.** It never considers age, gender, religion or similar traits.

## What should you expect from one?

Expect a list, not a pile. A good agent would rather send 14 strong people than pad to 20, and tells you which requirement is narrowing the search so you can decide whether it can bend.

Expect people who didn't apply. Most candidates an agent finds aren't actively looking, which is the point: they're the people a job post doesn't reach.

Expect speed on the first list and judgement after it. The first list shows how the agent read your role. Your feedback is what makes the second and third lists sharp.
