LinkedIn Recruiter is a search tool: your team pays per seat, writes searches, reads profiles and sends InMails. Kello is a sourcing service: you send a job description, and Kello reads profiles for you and returns a list of up to 20 people with why each one fits, usually in about two hours, then reaches out to the people you approve.

Last reviewed: October 2026

**TL;DR**

- LinkedIn Recruiter is priced per seat; Kello is one flat fee per role (₹75,000 in India, US$3,000 elsewhere, excluding taxes), with no per-seat pricing.
- With LinkedIn Recruiter, your team does the searching and outreach. With Kello, Kello does the sourcing and a named Kello person does the outreach.
- Kello reads each profile for meaning: what the employer actually does, company stage, early promotions, whether your role is a step up.
- You can use both. Kello doesn't ask for exclusivity.

## Search vs reading

A search returns people whose profiles contain the right words. That's useful, but titles and keywords hide most of what matters. "Head of Sales" at a 15-person startup and at a 5,000-person company are different jobs.

Kello reads each profile the way a senior recruiter would. It starts with your company: what you do, how you've grown, the kind of people you've hired. Then for each candidate it looks at what each employer actually does, how big the company was when they joined and when they left, whether they were promoted early compared with peers, and what they worked on. It leaves out people for whom your role would be a step down, and weighs how likely they are to move.

For engineering and AI roles, Kello also looks at public work, such as code shipped, research published or products launched, when the role calls for it.

## When is LinkedIn Recruiter the better choice?

- **You have a full-time sourcing team** that wants to run its own searches every day across many roles.
- **You want to browse**, not get a recommendation: exploring a market, mapping a competitor's team, or keeping a running talent pool.
- **Your team's strength is InMail outreach** in its own voice from its own accounts.
- **You hire high volumes of similar roles** where the search is well understood and the bottleneck is contacting people.

## When is Kello the better choice?

- You don't have the hours to search, read and message for every role.
- The role is hard to search for by title, or the right people come from unexpected backgrounds.
- You want someone else to run outreach, including WhatsApp in India, and only bring you people who are interested and meet your must-haves.
- You want the whole team, including hiring managers, to review a list without buying seats.

## When should you use both?

Use LinkedIn Recruiter for the searches your team runs well, and send Kello the roles that are hard, urgent or outside your team's usual market. If you're already talking to someone Kello lists, tell Kello or mark them No and they won't be contacted. If you'd rather reach a Kello candidate yourself, ask for their contact details: they're included in the role fee, with no credits.

## FAQ

### Where does Kello find candidates?

Kello searches public professional information across 836M+ people and reads each profile itself. Outreach is done by a named Kello person from their own LinkedIn, email and WhatsApp accounts, never yours.

### Do I need a LinkedIn Recruiter seat to use Kello?

No. Kello sends your list as a link anyone on your team can open, review and mark. There's no per-seat pricing.

### Is Kello cheaper than LinkedIn Recruiter?

They're priced differently. LinkedIn Recruiter is a per-seat cost plus your team's time. Kello is a flat fee per role that covers sourcing, outreach and first conversations for 3 months. The better comparison is cost per role filled, including hours.

### Can Kello message candidates for me?

Yes. Kello reaches out to everyone you mark Yes, then Maybe, never anyone marked No. Your company's name is revealed only once a candidate says they're open to a role like yours.

### Will I see the same people I'd find on LinkedIn Recruiter?

Some, yes. The difference is which ones and why. Kello reads for fit and likelihood to move, and looks for several kinds of people who could succeed, so the list isn't one profile repeated 20 times.
