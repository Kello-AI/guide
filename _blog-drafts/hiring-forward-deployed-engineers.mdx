A forward deployed engineer (FDE) is a customer-facing software engineer who embeds with customers to deploy, integrate and adapt a product to their real systems and workflows. To hire one, look for people who have shipped production code and also worked directly with users or customers. - FDEs combine strong engineering with customer-facing work: scoping, building, deploying and owning outcomes on site or alongside customers.
- Titles vary. Strong candidates may be called solutions engineers, founding engineers, implementation engineers or plain software engineers.
- The best signals are shipped code, direct customer exposure, and comfort with ambiguity and context-switching.

## What does a forward deployed engineer do?

An FDE works at the point where a product meets a customer. Typical work includes:

- Understanding a customer's problem, systems and data, often on site or in close contact.
- Building integrations, workflows and custom pieces so the product works in that environment.
- Getting deployments live and owning whether they succeed.
- Feeding what they learn back to the product and engineering teams.

The role is common at companies selling complex software or AI products to large customers, where each deployment needs real engineering.

## How is an FDE different from a solutions engineer or software engineer?

A solutions or sales engineer usually supports the sale: demos, technical questions, proofs of concept. A product software engineer usually builds for many customers at once. An FDE writes production code for a specific customer's deployment and stays with it after the sale. The lines blur, which is why titles alone are a weak guide.

## What signals matter when hiring an FDE?

In general, look for evidence of both halves of the job.

**Engineering depth.** Production code they've shipped, systems they've integrated, ownership of something that ran in the real world. Public work, such as open-source contributions or launched projects, can help show this, but many strong engineers have little public work.

**Customer-facing experience.** Time spent with users or customers: implementation, consulting with an engineering focus, technical support escalations, or being an early engineer at a startup where everyone talks to customers.

**Range and ownership.** Comfort moving across the stack and across problems, and a record of seeing things through rather than handing them off.

**Context they've worked in.** Experience at companies selling to similar customers, or in similar environments, shortens ramp-up.

**Practicalities.** Travel, time on customer sites and location often matter more for FDEs than for other engineers. Put them in the brief.

## Where do good FDE candidates come from?

Often from several kinds of background at once: early engineers at startups, solutions or implementation engineers who write real code, engineers from consulting or services firms, former founders, and product engineers who've worked closely with customers. A good list includes more than one of these, so you can see which shape fits your team.

## How do I write an FDE brief?

Add what a typical FDE job description leaves out:

- Who your customers are and what a deployment looks like.
- How much travel or on-site time to expect, and where.
- The stack or kinds of systems they'll integrate with.
- Must-haves vs nice-to-haves, such as years of production engineering or prior customer-facing work.
- Budget, and companies to hire from or avoid.
- LinkedIn profiles of FDEs you'd hire in a heartbeat, so Kello can find more people like them.
