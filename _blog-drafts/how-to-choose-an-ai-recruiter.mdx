To choose an AI recruiter, ask who does the work, how fast you see candidates, whether you can see the reasoning behind each one, how it treats personal traits, who contacts candidates, and exactly when and how you pay. This checklist gives ten questions to ask any vendor, what a good answer looks like, and Kello's answer to each.

**TL;DR**

- Ask for specifics: times, list sizes, fees, and what happens when a search comes up short.
- Insist on seeing why each candidate was chosen. A list without reasons is hard to trust or correct.
- Check who sends outreach, from whose accounts, and whether a person stands behind it.
- Check the pricing mechanics: when you pay, what's included, and whether a fee applies later.
- Try it on a real role. Kello's first role is free, one per company.

## The 10 questions

### 1. Who does the sourcing work, you or my team?

**A good answer:** clear about the split. Some products are tools your team operates; others do the sourcing for you.

**Kello:** Kello does the sourcing. You send a job description and decide who to talk to. Kello is the sourcer on your team, working alongside your recruiters.

### 2. How fast will I see candidates?

**A good answer:** a measured time to the first list and to interviews, not "instantly".

**Kello:** The first list arrives in about two hours. Next lists usually follow within a few hours of feedback. Most roles have candidates ready to interview within 7 days.

### 3. Can I see why each candidate was chosen?

**A good answer:** reasons specific to your role for every person, traceable to the candidate's own profile, plus how the search was approached.

**Kello:** Each person comes with a highlight, why they fit your role, and one thing worth checking. The list page also explains how Kello read the role, what it learned from your past hires, the kinds of people it looked for and what it relaxed and why.

### 4. How do you read a profile?

**A good answer:** more than keyword or title matching. Company context, career trajectory, and whether the role is a step up.

**Kello:** Kello starts with your company and the people you've hired, then reads each profile for meaning: what each employer does, company size and stage when they joined and left, early promotions, what they worked on. It leaves out people for whom your role is a step down. For engineering and AI roles it also looks at public work when the role calls for it.

### 5. How do you handle age, gender and other personal traits?

**A good answer:** they're never considered, and a request for one is turned into a professional signal.

**Kello:** Kello never considers age, gender, religion, race, marital status or similar personal traits. If a brief asks for one, Kello uses a professional signal instead, such as an experience range instead of an age.

### 6. How does it learn from my feedback?

**A good answer:** your decisions and reasons visibly change the next list.

**Kello:** Mark Yes, Maybe or No and add a note, or reply in plain words like "more B2B SaaS". Kello studies the Yes and No profiles in depth to learn your team's taste. Every new list has only new people.

### 7. Who contacts candidates, and from whose accounts?

**A good answer:** clear about who sends messages, which channels, and whether a person stands behind them.

**Kello:** A named Kello person reaches out from their own LinkedIn, email and WhatsApp, never your accounts. AI gives them context on each candidate so the message fits the person. Kello contacts only people you mark Yes or Maybe, and reveals your company name only once a candidate says they're open to a role like yours.

### 8. How do you charge, and when do I pay?

**A good answer:** a price you can calculate before you start, and a clear trigger for the invoice.

**Kello:** One flat fee per role: ₹75,000 in India or US$3,000 elsewhere, excluding taxes, at any seniority. Invoiced when your first list arrives, payable within 30 days. No subscription, no retainer, no minimum.

### 9. What happens if you can't find anyone?

**A good answer:** a short list comes with an explanation, and you don't pay for nothing.

**Kello:** If a list is short, Kello says which requirement is narrowing the search. If Kello finds nobody, there's no charge. If you said Yes to people and Kello couldn't connect you with even one, the fee is returned.

### 10. Who owns the candidates and my data?

**A good answer:** you keep candidates with no later fee, and your data isn't used to train models or shared.

**Kello:** You keep everyone Kello finds and their contact details, with no placement fee, even if you hire later. No exclusivity. Your job description, feedback and data are used only to get your own lists right, never to train Kello's algorithms, and never shared with other customers.

## What red flags should you watch for?

- Speed claims with no measured number behind them.
- A list with no reasons, or reasons that could apply to anyone.
- Outreach sent from your accounts without you reviewing who gets contacted.
- A price that's only clear after the hire.
- Fees that apply to candidates you hire later from a list you already paid for.

## How do you test an AI recruiter?

Send a real, current role, ideally a hard one. Add what the job description doesn't say: location, budget, must-haves and companies to hire from or avoid. Then judge the first list on three things: would you talk to most of these people, do the reasons hold up, and did your feedback change the second list.

With Kello, the first role is free, one per company. No card, no contract and no invoice, even if you hire.
