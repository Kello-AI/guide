When a Kello list is off, the fastest fix is one plain line that says what's wrong and why, either as a note on the candidates you mark No or as an email reply. Kello reads that "why" alongside your Yes and No marks and adjusts the next list, which usually arrives within a few hours.

## What should I write?

Find what you're seeing in the left column. Copy the line on the right, edit it to fit, and send it as an email reply or add it as a note on the list page.

| What you see | What it usually means | What to tell Kello (copy this) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Candidates are too senior | The experience range or title level in the brief is wider than you meant | "Too senior. We need someone who still writes code daily, 5–8 years, not people managing managers." |
| Candidates are too junior | The brief undersold the scope, or the title reads lower than the real role | "Too junior. This person will own the function and report to the CEO. Look for people who have already led a team of 5+." |
| Wrong industry | The brief didn't say which industries matter or which to avoid | "No pharma or healthcare. Prefer B2B SaaS, ideally companies selling to mid-market." |
| Too few people in my city | Location was loose, or the city has a small pool and remote wasn't mentioned | "Bangalore is a must. Open to people willing to relocate from Pune or Hyderabad." |
| Good profiles, but they won't move | You want people more likely to say yes right now | "Several of these just joined new roles. Prioritise people who have been in their current role a while." |
| Great profiles, wrong function | The title is used differently across companies | "These are solutions engineers. We need people who build and ship product, then sit with customers. Engineering first." |
| I liked one person, I want more like them | One Yes is the clearest signal of your taste | "Love [name]. Their move from an early-stage startup into a larger company is exactly what we want. More like this." |
| Wrong kind of company | Company size or stage matters more than the brief said | "Too many large enterprises. We want people who have worked at startups between 20 and 200 people." |
| Strong on paper, but my hiring manager said no | The real must-haves aren't written down yet | "Hiring manager wants hands-on experience with enterprise customers. That's a must-have, not a nice-to-have." |
| The whole list feels wrong | Kello may have misread the role | "This is a Director role, not a Manager role. Please restart on that basis." |

If Kello misread something basic, like the level or the city, Kello restarts on the corrected brief, even mid-search.

## Tips for writing useful "why" notes

Kello studies your Yes and No profiles in depth to learn your team's taste. A note makes the reason explicit, so Kello doesn't have to guess which part of a profile you reacted to.

### Name the reason, not the verdict

A No already says you don't want them. The note should say why.

- Weak: "Not a fit."
- Good: "Not a fit. Only agency experience, we need someone who has been in-house."

### Say whether it's firm

Kello asks before relaxing your requirements. Telling it upfront which ones can bend saves a round.

- Weak: "Should have 8+ years."
- Good: "8+ years is flexible if they have led a team. Leading a team is firm."

### Tell Kello what you liked, too

Yes notes matter as much as No notes. They show Kello what to look for more of.

- Weak: "Yes."
- Good: "Yes. Built the data team from scratch at a 50-person company. That's the story we want."

### Pass on what you learned in interviews

After a first conversation, a line of interview feedback is often the clearest signal of all.

- Weak: "Didn't work out."
- Good: "Strong on systems design, but couldn't explain trade-offs to a non-technical customer. Customer communication matters more than we said."
