Your Kello list arrives as a link to your list page with up to 20 people who fit your role. Each person comes with a highlight, the reasons they fit your role, and one thing worth checking, and you mark each one Yes, Maybe or No. If not enough people meet the bar, Kello sends fewer and tells you which requirement is narrowing the search, and you never get an empty list.

Every highlight and reason comes from the candidate's own profile or Kello's data on companies and people. Kello doesn't invent them.

## How does the list arrive?

As a link to your Kello list page, usually about two hours after you send the role. You can share the link with anyone involved in the hire.

## What does each candidate show?

Each person on the list has:

- **Name, current role and company, location and experience.**
- **A highlight.** What stands out about this person.
- **Why this candidate.** The reasons this person fits *your* role, written in plain words.
- **Worth checking.** One thing to verify before or during a first conversation.

"Why this candidate" is written for your role, not a generic summary. The same person would read differently on another company's list.

## How do I mark candidates?

Mark each person **Yes**, **Maybe** or **No**. Add a short note on why if you can: "too senior", "love this one", "wrong industry".

Your marks do two things:

1. **They decide outreach.** Kello reaches out to everyone you mark Yes, then everyone you mark Maybe. Never anyone you mark No.
2. **They shape the next list.** Kello studies your Yes and No profiles in depth to learn your team's taste. Notes make the reasons explicit.

See [feedback](/kello/feedback) for more.

## What does "how Kello approached your search" show?

The list page also explains Kello's thinking:

- How it read your role.
- What it learned from your past hires.
- The kinds of people it looked for.
- What it relaxed, and why.

Read this first. If Kello misread something, reply with the correction ("it's a Director role", "Pune, not Bangalore") and Kello restarts on the corrected brief.

## Are these people looking for a job?

Most aren't actively looking. Nobody on your list applied, and nobody is contacted until you say yes.

## What if I already know someone on the list?

Tell Kello and they won't be contacted, or simply mark them No. If they're already in your hiring process, say so.

## Can I share the list?

Yes. Share the list link with your hiring manager or anyone else involved. Anyone with the link can review and mark candidates. There's no per-seat pricing. See [your team](/kello/team).

## Can I download the list?

Yes. Download it from the list link.

## Why are there fewer than 20 people?

Because not enough people met the bar for your role. Each list has **up to 20** people, not exactly 20.

When a list is short, Kello tells you which requirement is narrowing the search. It might be a minimum years of experience, a single city, a budget, or a specific industry. Only you know which of these is truly firm.

## Why doesn't Kello fill the list anyway?

Because a padded list wastes your time. Kello would rather send you 14 strong people than 20 with six who don't fit.

## What happens if nobody fits?

You never get an empty list. Kello works through it in this order:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Kello checks its own assumptions">
    If an assumption Kello made is the cause, such as reading a nice-to-have as a must-have, Kello adjusts and searches again.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Kello asks you one clear question">
    If one of your requirements is the cause, Kello asks you about it directly. For example: "Is the 8-year minimum firm?"
  </Step>
  <Step title="Someone from the Kello team steps in">
    If the search is still stuck, a person from the Kello team works on it with you.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What should I do with a short list?

1. **Read why it's short.** The list page explains what Kello relaxed and what is narrowing the search.
2. **Decide if the requirement can bend.** Reply in plain words.
3. **Mark the people you have.** Yes, Maybe or No, with a note. Your marks shape the next list even when this one is short.

Example replies:

- "8 years is flexible. 6+ is fine if they've led a team."
- "Open to people in Pune or Hyderabad who'd relocate."
- "Budget can stretch 15% for the right person."
- "Fintech is a nice-to-have, not a must-have."

Kello's next list usually arrives within a few hours of your feedback.

## Does a role in two cities get two lists?

No. A role open in two cities is one role. List both locations and the list stays up to 20 people across both. If you want a full list for each city, send each city as a separate role.

## How do I avoid a short list next time?

- Split must-haves from nice-to-haves in your brief.
- Say which requirements can bend.
- Share a few LinkedIn profiles of people you'd hire.

See [write a great brief](/kello/write-a-great-brief).
