A great Kello brief is your job description plus the few things it doesn't say: location, budget, which requirements are must-haves and which are nice-to-haves, companies to hire from or avoid, and a few LinkedIn profiles of people you'd hire in a heartbeat. Those few lines decide most of what lands in your first list.

You don't need a polished JD. A PDF, a Word doc, a link to the job post or pasted text all work. No JD? Send the role name and a few lines on who you need.

## What should a brief include?

- **The JD**, in any format.
- **Location.** City, office days, or remote.
- **Budget.** A range, or "no fixed budget, use market rates".
- **Must-haves vs nice-to-haves.** What truly matters, and what can bend.
- **Companies to hire from or avoid.**
- **People you'd hire in a heartbeat.** A few LinkedIn profiles. See [how to share example profiles](#how-do-i-share-example-profiles).
- **Your website**, if your company name is shared with others.

## Why split must-haves from nice-to-haves?

Most JDs list ten requirements with equal weight. Kello needs to know which two or three are firm.

When not enough people meet the bar, Kello tells you which requirement is narrowing the search. If you've already said what can bend, Kello can make a better call and you get a stronger list.

## Do I need to give a location and budget?

Yes, if you have them. If either is missing, Kello asks one short question before searching. A one-line reply is enough.

A role open in two cities is one role: list both locations and the list stays up to 20 people. Want a full list for each city? Send each city as a separate role.

## Should I name companies to target or avoid?

It helps. Name companies whose people you'd like to meet, and companies you don't want to hire from, such as customers or partners.

Kello already starts with your company: what it does, how it has grown, and the kind of people it has hired. Your list of companies adds what Kello can't see from the outside.

## What makes an addition strong or weak?

Specific lines give Kello something to read profiles against. Vague ones don't.

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| "Competitive salary" | "Up to 40 LPA fixed" or "No fixed budget, use market rates" |
| "Good culture fit" | "Has worked in a team of under 20 engineers" |
| "Strong sales background" | "Has sold B2B SaaS to mid-market finance teams" |
| "Bangalore preferred" | "Bangalore, 3 days a week in office. Firm." |
| "Top companies" | "Would love people from fintech startups. Avoid our customers: `<names>`" |
| "Young, energetic team" | "3–6 years of experience" |

Kello never considers age, gender, religion, race, marital status or similar personal traits. If a brief asks for one, Kello uses a professional signal instead, such as an experience range in place of an age. See [data, privacy and fairness](/kello/trust).

## How do I share example profiles?

Sharing a few example profiles is the fastest way to show Kello your team's taste before the first list. Paste the LinkedIn profile links into your brief email to **source@kello.ai**, under a line that says what they are.

```text
People I'd hire in a heartbeat:
https://www.linkedin.com/in/...
https://www.linkedin.com/in/...
https://www.linkedin.com/in/...
```

## What does Kello learn from them?

Kello reads each example the same way it reads every candidate: for meaning, not keywords. It looks at what each of their employers actually does, the size and stage of those companies when they joined and when they left, how quickly they were promoted, and what they worked on.

Then it looks for what the examples share. Maybe they all joined companies early and stayed through a growth phase. Maybe they all moved from engineering into customer-facing work. Those patterns are often things a JD never says. Kello then finds more people like them.

## Who makes a good example?

- People on your team who are great at this job.
- Past colleagues you'd hire again.
- People at other companies you admire but can't hire.

If two quite different people would both succeed in the role, share both. Kello looks for several different kinds of people who could succeed, so the list isn't one profile repeated 20 times.

## Will the example people appear in my list?

No. They're examples, not candidates. Kello uses them to understand the role, and they won't be in your list.

## What if I don't have any examples?

That's fine. Example profiles are optional. Kello always starts with your company: what it does, its history and growth, its current team, and the kind of people it has hired. That alone gives the first list a strong starting point.

## Do my Yes marks work the same way?

Yes. Once your list arrives, every person you mark **Yes** or **No** teaches Kello the same way your examples did. Kello studies the Yes and No profiles in depth to learn your team's taste.

A short note on why ("love this one", "too senior") makes the reason explicit, so Kello doesn't have to guess. See [feedback](/kello/feedback).

## Can I send several roles in one email?

No. Send **one role per email**. Every role gets its own search and its own list.

## What if I get something wrong?

Reply with the correction, like "it's a Director role" or "Pune, not Bangalore". Kello restarts on the corrected brief, even mid-search.

Kello also confirms how it read the role within minutes of your email. That's the easiest moment to catch a misread.
